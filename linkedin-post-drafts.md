## Post 1: The IMG Reality Check

**Headline:** 3 things nobody tells you about doing medical residency in Germany

When I started researching the German Approbation pathway for non-EU doctors, I found plenty of official guides and government websites. What I didn't find: the real talk about what the process actually feels like.

Here's what stood out:

1. Language is the real filter, not the exams
B2 is the minimum on paper. But IMGs who breeze through the process are the ones who hit C1 before applying. The Fachsprachprüfung (medical German exam) tests specific clinical vocabulary that general language courses barely touch.

2. Document certification takes longer than the exams
Getting your medical degree attested, translated, and accepted by the Landesprüfungsamt takes 2-3 months in most cases. Some home countries require in-person document pickup. Start this before anything else.

3. The bottleneck is the oral exam
The Approbation oral exam tests your ability to think clinically in German. Even with strong written German, many IMGs struggle here. Preparation should focus on doctor-patient dialogues and case presentations out loud.

## Post 2: Goethe B2 vs C1 - What You Actually Need

**Headline:** B2 or C1 for German medical residency? The honest answer

Every week I see IMGs asking "Is B2 enough for Approbation?"

Technically yes. Realistically no.

Here's the breakdown:

B2 gets you:
- Application accepted
- Written knowledge exam passed
- Basic patient communication

C1 gets you:
- Oral exam confidence
- Treatment discussion with patients
- Integration into clinical teams
- Better job offers after licensing

The real goal isn't the certificate. It's being comfortable enough in German to handle this situation:

"A 65-year-old patient with acute dyspnea, chest tightness radiating to the left arm, and a history of hypertension. Explain your differential diagnosis in German."

Most B2 holders freeze. C1 holders work through it.

My recommendation: target C1 from day one. Use medical German resources (Arztbrief templates, patient dialogue practice) alongside general language study. The Fachsprachprüfung demands both.

## Post 3: Which Specialties Are IMG-Friendly in Germany?

**Headline:** The truth about specialty choice as an IMG in Germany

Not all specialties treat IMGs equally in the German system.

The ones that need doctors most (and are most willing to sponsor/work with IMGs):

High access:
- Internal Medicine (all subspecialties)
- General Practice
- Psychiatry and Psychotherapy
- Anesthesiology
- Neurology
- Pathology

Medium access:
- Pediatrics
- OB/GYN
- Orthopedics
- Ophthalmology
- Dermatology

Tougher for IMGs:
- Surgery (language-heavy OR environment)
- ENT
- Radiology (competitive, limited spots)
- Cardiothoracic Surgery

My advice: start in a high-access field. Get your German clinical experience. Once you're in the system and your language is at native level, switching specialties later is much easier.

A common path:
Year 1-2: Internal Medicine or GP
Year 3+: Move into subspecialty or competitive field

The German system rewards persistence more than prestige-hunting. Show up, work well, speak good German, and doors open.

## Post 4: The 6-Month Approbation Timeline

**Headline:** Realistic 6-month roadmap to German Approbation for non-EU IMGs

I mapped this out based on actual IMG experiences going through the process.

Month 1:
- Start Goethe B2 course (or continue existing German study)
- Contact Landesprüfungsamt to confirm requirements
- Begin document collection: degree, passport, CV, proof of language

Month 2:
- Submit documents for certification/translation
- Continue language study (aim for B2 by end of month)
- Research which Bundesland has the fastest processing

Month 3:
- Documents being processed (this is the waiting game)
- Start Fachsprachprüfung preparation
- Begin collecting medical German vocabulary by organ system

Month 4:
- Receive document approval
- Register for knowledge exam (Kenntnisprüfung)
- Intensive exam prep with German medical textbooks

Month 5:
- Take written knowledge exam
- Start oral exam preparation
- Practice case presentations in German with a partner

Month 6:
- Oral exam
- License issued
- Begin job applications

The real trick: do language prep and document collection in parallel. Most people who take 12 months wasted 4-5 months waiting for documents before starting German study.

## Post 5: Why Germany Over US/UK for IMGs?

**Headline:** 5 reasons I recommend Germany over the US match for IMGs

I work with doctors who've done both paths. Here's the comparison that comes up most:

1. No USMLE equivalent
German knowledge exam is one test, not three. Cost is a few hundred euros vs thousands for USMLE.

2. Better work-life balance
German resident contracts average 40-42 hours/week. Salary starts around 55k EUR. Overtime is paid.

3. No match lottery
The US match is a stressful algorithm. In Germany, you apply directly to hospitals. If they want you and you meet requirements, you get in.

4. Faster pathway
US: 12-18 months prep + residency match + visa
Germany: 6-9 months for Approbation + start working

5. Citizenship path
After 5-6 years of residency, you can apply for German citizenship. EU passport opens all of Europe.

Downsides are real too: German bureaucracy, language barrier, and winters if you're from a warm country. But overall Germany deserves way more attention from IMGs than it gets.
