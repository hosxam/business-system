## 10 Ready-to-Post Facebook Posts

### Post 1 (Educational)
"The single biggest time-waster in the Approbation process is waiting for documents. Start collecting these today, not after you pass your language exam: your medical degree (notarized), your transcript, your medical license, and your certificate of good standing from your medical council."

### Post 2 (Discussion)
"Question for those already working in Germany: if you could go back and change one thing about how you approached the process, what would it be?"

### Post 3 (Educational)
"Not all German states process Approbation applications at the same speed. Sachsen and Thüringen take 2-3 months. Bayern can take 5+. The Approbation is valid nationwide, so you can apply in a fast state and work anywhere."

### Post 4 (Observation)
"I've noticed most IMGs focus on the exam and underestimate the logistics. Where you'll live, how you'll register, what health insurance you need. These take just as much time as studying."

### Post 5 (Educational)
"The FSP exam format: you'll get a patient case, take history (Anamnese), perform an exam (Untersuchung), suggest diagnostics (Diagnostik), propose treatment (Therapie), and explain it to the patient (Aufklärung). Practice talking through this sequence."

### Post 6 (Discussion)
"How did you prepare for the Kenntnisprüfung? What resources actually helped?"

### Post 7 (Helpful)
"Goethe B2 and C1 test general German. The FSP tests clinical German. They're different skills. Even with C1, plan 4-6 weeks of medical German prep before the FSP."

### Post 8 (Observation)
"Some Landesärztekammern are more IMG-friendly than others. If you hit a wall with one, consider whether switching states is possible for your situation."

### Post 9 (Educational)
"Five German medical terms to learn before day one: Anamnese (history), Aufklärung (informed consent), Überweisung (referral), Einwilligung (consent), Entlassungsbrief (discharge summary)."

### Post 10 (Community)
"Anyone else currently waiting on their Approbation? Comment where you applied and how long you've been waiting. Let's compare."

## What to Avoid in All Groups

- Direct sales language ("buy my product", "check out my shop")
- Repeatedly posting the same content
- Linking to Ko-fi in group posts (put in comments only when appropriate)
- Copy-pasting the same comment across multiple posts
- DMing group members with offers
- Responding to every single post (looks like a bot)
- Excessive self-reference ("I made a guide about this")
- Making claims you can't back up ("guaranteed approval")
- Negative comments about other doctors' choices
- Arguing with other commenters

## When to Mention the Free Roadmap

Only in these specific situations:
1. Someone asks directly "where can I find a guide/checklist/resource?"
2. Someone says "I wish there was something that covered all states"
3. Your own post gets engagement and someone asks "how did you find this out?"
4. A mod or admin asks for resources
5. In a "what resources helped you?" thread (comment, not top-level post)

## When Not to Mention It

- Your first 3 comments in any group
- In response to a medical question (refer to their doctor)
- In a thread where someone is clearly struggling (offer help, not a product)
- In a subreddit/FB group that bans self-promotion (read rules first)
