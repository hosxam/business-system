### Week 1 Posts

**Post 1 (Done):** 3 things nobody tells you about medical residency in Germany

**Post 2:**
"The hardest part of the German Approbation isn't the exam. It's the waiting. 4-8 weeks for document authentication. 2-4 weeks for application acknowledgment. 2-4 weeks for exam results. The process requires patience as much as preparation."

**Post 3:**
"Goethe B2 vs C1 for IMGs going to Germany.

B2 gets you: application accepted, basic communication, written exam passed.

C1 gets you: oral exam confidence, job interview success, patient trust.

Don't stop at B2 if you can help it."

**Post 4:**
"I've been comparing Approbation processing times across German states. The range is wider than most IMGs realize.

Sachsen, Thüringen: 2-3 months
NRW, Berlin: 3-5 months
Bayern, Baden-Württemberg: 4-6+ months

Your choice of state matters more than your qualifications in terms of timeline."

### Week 2 Posts

**Post 5:**
"Five documents every IMG needs before applying to Germany:

1. Medical degree (notarized)
2. Transcript of records
3. Medical license from home country
4. Certificate of good standing
5. Goethe B2/C1 certificate

Start collecting these now. Some take 6-8 weeks."

**Post 6:**
"The Kenntnisprüfung is different from any exam you've taken before. It's not a written multiple-choice test. It's a case-based oral exam where you talk through a patient scenario in German. The best preparation is practicing out loud."

**Post 7:**
"What IMGs should know about German hospital interviews:

They'll test your clinical German vocabulary. They'll ask about your favorite case. They'll want to know why Germany specifically. Prepare structured answers in German, not generic responses."

**Post 8:**
"The one question every German hospital asks in interviews: 'Warum möchten Sie in Deutschland arbeiten?'

Have a real, personal answer. 'Better opportunities' is generic. Mention something specific about the German healthcare system or the hospital itself."

### Week 3 Posts

**Post 9:**
"I've noticed most IMGs focus on the exam and ignore the logistics. Housing, registration, health insurance, bank account. These take real time and effort. Plan for them."

**Post 10:**
"German salary expectations for doctors:

Assistenzarzt Year 1: ~55K EUR
Year 3: ~65K EUR
Year 5+: ~75-85K EUR

Facharzt: 80-120K EUR depending on specialty and state.

Salaries are lower than the US. Work-life balance and benefits are better."

**Post 11:**
"Fachsprachprüfung tip: The exam usually starts with a patient presentation. Prepare a template in German. Name, age, presenting complaint, history, findings. Practice this sequence until it's automatic."

**Post 12:**
"Your first month as an Assistenzarzt in Germany:

Week 1: Onboarding, IT systems, meet your team
Week 2: Start seeing patients with supervision
Week 3: First independent tasks
Week 4: First on-call shift

You'll feel lost. Everyone does. It gets better."

### Week 4 Posts

**Post 13:**
