# Approbation Document Checklist

## The Complete Guide to Getting Your German Medical License

### Overview

The Approbation process requires a specific set of documents, each with its own authentication and translation requirements. Missing one document or using the wrong authentication method can delay your application by 3-6 months.

This checklist covers everything you need.

### Essential Documents (Must Have)

**Personal Identification**
- [ ] Valid passport (certified copy)
- [ ] Birth certificate (certified copy + apostille)
- [ ] Marriage certificate (if applicable, certified copy)
- [ ] Proof of name change (if applicable)
- [ ] CV in German (Lebenslauf mit Foto)
- [ ] Certificate of Good Standing from your medical council

**Medical Qualifications**
- [ ] Medical degree certificate (original + certified translation)
- [ ] Medical license/registration from your home country
- [ ] Internship completion certificate
- [ ] Residency/specialty training certificates (if any)
- [ ] Transcript of medical school grades
- [ ] Logbook of procedures (if available)

### Secondary Documents

**Employment History**
- [ ] Proof of previous employment (Arbeitszeugnisse or equivalent)
- [ ] List of previous positions with dates
- [ ] Reference letters from supervisors

**Language**
- [ ] Goethe B2 or C1 certificate
- [ ] Telc B2 or C1 certificate (alternative)
- [ ] Medical German certificate (Fachsprachprufung, if already taken)

### Authentication Requirements by Country

**Hague Convention Countries (Apostille)**
Countries that signed the 1961 Hague Convention need apostille from the issuing authority in your home country. No further embassy legalization needed.

**Non-Hague Convention Countries**
Documents need legalization by the German embassy in your country of issue.

### Translation Requirements

Every document not already in German must be translated by a state-sworn translator (beeidigter Ubersetzer). The translation must include:
- Translator's stamp and signature
- Certification that the translation is accurate
- Date of translation

### State-by-State Variations

| State | Key Difference | Processing Time |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| Bayern | Requires C1 from start | 3-6 months |
| NRW | Accepts B2, C1 within 6 months | 2-4 months |
| Berlin | Most international-friendly | 3-5 months |
| Hessen | Accepts B2 | 2-4 months |
| Baden-Wurttemberg | Requires C1 from start | 4-6 months |

### Common Delay Points

1. **Certificate of Good Standing expires** - Most are valid for only 3-6 months
2. **Wrong apostille authority** - Must be from the correct government office
3. **Uncertified translation** - Must be from a state-sworn translator
4. **Incomplete application** - Check everything before submitting
5. **Missing document** - One missing item stops the entire process
