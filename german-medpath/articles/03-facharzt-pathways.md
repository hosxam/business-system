# Facharzt in Germany — Your Specialty Options

*Which specialties are in demand, how the German training system works, and how to choose the right path as an IMG.*

---

## The German System: How It Works

After getting your Approbation, every doctor starts as an Assistenzarzt (resident). You spend 5-6 years in structured training (Weiterbildung) before taking the Facharztprüfung (specialty exam). Unlike the US system, German residency isn't a competitive match. You apply directly to individual hospitals.

**Structure:**
- **Common trunk (3-6 months):** Every specialty starts with a foundation in internal medicine
- **Core training (4-5 years):** Rotations through subspecialties
- **Optional rotations:** You can spend 6-12 months in related fields
- **Facharztprüfung:** Oral exam before your state medical chamber

**Salary progression:**
- Year 1: ~55,000 EUR (approx 200,000 AED)
- Year 3: ~65,000 EUR 
- Year 5 (Facharzt): ~75,000-85,000 EUR
- Oberarzt (senior): ~100,000-140,000 EUR

## In-Demand Specialties for IMGs

### 1. Internal Medicine (Innere Medizin)

**Demand:** Very High
**Training length:** 5 years
**Language requirement:** B2+ (C1 recommended)
**Job availability:** Every hospital in Germany

Internal medicine is the most straightforward path. There are more open positions than applicants in most regions. This is the specialty where IMGs have the highest success rate.

**Subspecialty options after core training:**
- Cardiology (highest demand)
- Gastroenterology
- Nephrology (excellent for IMGs, high demand)
- Pneumology
- Endocrinology
- Rheumatology
- Hematology/Oncology

### 2. Psychiatry (Psychiatrie und Psychotherapie)

**Demand:** High
**Training length:** 5 years
**Language requirement:** C1 minimum
**Job availability:** Very good

Psychiatry is less competitive than surgery or anesthesia. The language demands are higher (you need to understand patients deeply), but the workload is generally lighter than internal medicine. Many IMGs from non-EU countries find positions here quickly.

### 3. Anesthesiology (Anästhesiologie)

**Demand:** High
**Training length:** 5 years
**Language requirement:** B2+
**Job availability:** Excellent in smaller hospitals

Anesthesia work is procedure-focused with less patient conversation than other fields. The language barrier is less of an issue here. Night shifts are frequent but the pay is good.

### 4. Neurology (Neurologie)

**Demand:** Medium-High
**Training length:** 5 years
**Language requirement:** C1
**Job availability:** Good in cities, excellent in rural areas

Neurology has a well-defined exam path and clear progression. The subject matter is fascinating if you enjoy clinical puzzles. Stroke units in particular are always understaffed.

### 5. General Practice (Allgemeinmedizin)

**Demand:** Very High
**Training length:** 5 years
**Language requirement:** C1 (you'll be primary contact for patients)
**Job availability:** Extremely high, especially in rural areas

Germany faces a GP shortage that will worsen as the current generation retires. This is the most secure long-term career choice. Training includes 2 years of hospital rotations and 1.5 years in an outpatient practice.

## Less DEMAND but Still Accessible

### Surgery (Chirurgie)
- **Demand:** Medium
- **Competition:** High for university hospitals
- **Note:** German surgical training is long (6 years) and hierarchical. IMGs face more barriers here.
- **Best bet:** Unfallchirurgie (trauma) or Viszeralchirurgie at smaller hospitals

### OB/GYN (Frauenheilkunde)
- **Demand:** Medium
- **Cultural sensitivity:** Female IMGs have an advantage (many patients prefer female doctors)
- **Note:** Strong German required for patient communication

### Pediatrics (Kinder- und Jugendmedizin)
- **Demand:** Medium
- **Language:** High level of German needed (communicating with children + parents)
- **Emotional toll:** High burnout rate. Know what you're signing up for.

## Regional Demand Differences

| Region | Best Specialties for IMGs | Reason |
|--------|--------------------------|--------|
| Bayern | Innere Medizin, Psychiatrie | High hospital density, more competition |
| Berlin | Alle specialties | Largest IMG community |
| NRW | Innere Medizin, Allgemeinmedizin | Highest concentration of hospitals |
| Sachsen | All specialties | Severe doctor shortage |
| Mecklenburg-Vorpommern | All specialties | Most severe shortage, easiest entry |
| Baden-Württemberg | Anästhesie, Psychiatrie | Higher living standards, more applicants |

**Strategy:** Start in a shortage area (east Germany, rural), get your Facharzt, then relocate if you want.

## How to Choose

### Ask yourself:

1. **How good is your German?** If B2, choose Anästhesie or Innere. If C1, consider Psychiatrie or Allgemeinmedizin.
2. **How much patient contact do you want?** Less contact = Anästhesie, Radiology. More contact = Allgemeinmedizin, Psychiatrie.
3. **What lifestyle do you want?** Anästhesie has shift work but clear boundaries. Innere has the most documentation burden. Allgemeinmedizin offers flexibility.
4. **Will you want to work in your home country again?** Choose a specialty that's equally recognized internationally (Innere Medizin, Anästhesie).

## How to Start

1. Get your Approbation first
2. Decide on 2-3 specialties you'd accept
3. Search for "Assistenzarzt [specialty]" + "zum [month]" on Indeed.de
4. Apply to 20-30 positions simultaneously
5. Accept the first reasonable offer (you can change after 6 months)
6. Your first year is probation (Probezeit) in all states

**You don't need to know what you want to do forever.** German doctors change specialties frequently in the first 2 years. The initial choice is not binding.
