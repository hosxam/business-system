Facharzt in Germany — Your Specialty Options

Which specialties are in demand, how training works, and how to choose the right path as an IMG.

The German System: How It Works

After your Approbation, every doctor starts as an Assistenzarzt (resident). You spend 5-6 years in structured training (Weiterbildung) before taking the Facharztprüfung (specialty exam). Unlike the US system, German residency is not a competitive match. You apply directly to individual hospitals.

Structure: Common trunk (3-6 months foundation in internal medicine), core training (4-5 years rotations through subspecialties), optional rotations (6-12 months in related fields), and the Facharztprüfung (oral exam before your state medical chamber).

Salary progression: Year 1 Assistenzarzt ~55,000 EUR, Year 3 ~65,000 EUR, Facharzt ~75,000-85,000 EUR, Oberarzt ~100,000-140,000 EUR.

In-Demand Specialties for IMGs

1. Internal Medicine (Innere Medizin)

Demand: Very High · Training: 5 years · Language: B2+ (C1 recommended)

Internal medicine is the most straightforward path. There are more open positions than applicants in most regions. This is the specialty where IMGs have the highest success rate.

Subspecialty options: Cardiology, Gastroenterology, Nephrology (excellent for IMGs), Pneumology, Endocrinology, Rheumatology, Hematology/Oncology.

2. Psychiatry (Psychiatrie und Psychotherapie)

Demand: High · Training: 5 years · Language: C1 minimum

Less competitive than surgery or anesthesia. Higher language demands but lighter workload than internal medicine. Many IMGs find positions quickly here.

3. Anesthesiology (Anästhesiologie)

Demand: High · Training: 5 years · Language: B2+

Procedure-focused with less patient conversation. The language barrier is less of an issue here. Night shifts are frequent but pay is good.

4. General Practice (Allgemeinmedizin)

Demand: Very High · Training: 5 years · Language: C1

Germany faces a GP shortage. This is the most secure long-term career choice. Training includes 2 years of hospital rotations and 1.5 years in an outpatient practice.

Less Demand but Still Accessible

Surgery (Chirurgie)

Long training (6 years) and hierarchical. IMGs face more barriers. Best bet is Unfallchirurgie or Viszeralchirurgie at smaller hospitals.

OB/GYN (Frauenheilkunde)

Requires strong German. Female IMGs have an advantage culturally.

Pediatrics (Kinder- und Jugendmedizin)

High language demands. High burnout rate. Rewarding if you are committed.
