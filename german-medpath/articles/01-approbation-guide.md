# How to Get Your Approbation in 6 Months

*A realistic month-by-month timeline for international medical graduates. What to do, when to start, and where IMGs waste the most time.*

---

## Before You Start

The Approbation process takes most IMGs 9-18 months. Six months is aggressive but doable if you already have B2 German and all your documents ready. If you're starting from zero German, plan for 12-18 months.

**The single biggest time-waster** is waiting. Waiting for document authentication. Waiting for the Landesärztekammer to respond. Waiting for the next exam date. This timeline is built around parallel processing — do five things at once instead of waiting for one to finish.

---

## Month 1: Document Collection & Language Assessment

### Documents You Need Now

Start collecting these immediately. Some take 4-8 weeks to get.

- **Medical degree certificate** — notarized copy
- **Transcript of records** — detailed, with hours per subject
- **Medical license/registration** — from your home country
- **Passport** — valid for at least 2 years
- **Curriculum vitae** — in tabular German format (Lebenslauf)
- **Certificate of good standing** — from your medical council
- **Proof of language skills** — Goethe B2 minimum, C1 preferred
- **Certificate of employment** — for any clinical work done

### German Translation Everything

Every document needs a certified translation into German. Notarized translations cost 30-80 EUR per page. Get them done in your home country where it's cheaper. Budget around 200-400 EUR total.

### Language Assessment

If you only have B1 German, stop everything and focus on B2. You cannot start the Approbation process without B2 certification. Goethe B2 takes most learners 3-4 months of intensive study.

**If you have B2 already:** Start studying for C1 and Fachsprachprüfung simultaneously.

---

## Month 2: Authentication & Application

### Apostille / Legalization

This is where most delays happen. Check whether your home country is part of the Hague Apostille Convention:
- **Apostille countries** (150+): One stamp from your foreign ministry is enough
- **Non-Apostille countries**: You need chain legalization (ministry → embassy → German mission). This takes 2-3x longer

### Choose Your State (Bundesland)

Not all states are equal for IMGs. Current processing times:

| State | Processing Time | Language Requirement | Demand for IMGs |
|-------|----------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Bayern | 3-5 months | B2 | Medium |
| Berlin | 4-6 months | B2 | High |
| NRW | 2-4 months | B2 | High |
| Hessen | 3-5 months | C1 | Medium |
| Sachsen | 2-3 months | B2 | Very High |
| Thüringen | 2-3 months | B2 | Very High |

**Tip:** Apply in a state with fast processing (Sachsen, Thüringen, NRW). The Approbation is valid nationwide — you can work anywhere in Germany once you have it.

### Submit Your Application

Send your complete application packet to the Landesärztekammer of your chosen state. Include:
- Cover letter explaining your request
- All authenticated documents
- Language certificate
- CV
- Passport photo (biometric, 35x45mm)

Expect an acknowledgment in 2-4 weeks. If you don't hear back, call them. German bureaucracy responds to follow-ups.

---

## Month 3: Kenntnisprüfung Preparation

Once your documents are accepted, the Kammer will assess your equivalency. Most IMGs from non-EU countries are required to take the Kenntnisprüfung (knowledge exam).

### What the Exam Covers

It's a practical and oral exam covering:
- Internal medicine (30-40% of questions)
- Surgery (15-20%)
- Pharmacology and prescribing (10-15%)
- Emergency medicine (10-15%)
- Pediatrics, OB/GYN, psychiatry (remaining)

The exam is case-based. You'll be given clinical scenarios and asked to diagnose, manage, and prescribe. **This is not a theoretical exam — it tests what you'd do with a real patient.**

### Study Strategy

- **Use German medical textbooks:** "Klinikleitfaden" series or "Duale Reihe" for each specialty
- **Focus on German guidelines:** Leitlinien from AWMF (awmf.org) — these are the standards you'll be tested on
- **Practice speaking:** Get a tutor on iTalki or Preply who specializes in medical German
- **Do mock exams:** The format matters. Practice talking through cases out loud

### Common Failure Points

1. Not knowing German drug names (e.g., Paracetamol vs. Acetaminophen, Metamizol/Novalgesic)
2. Wrong dosage units (Germany uses mg/kg differently in some contexts)
3. Failing to ask about allergies (mandatory in German clinical practice)
4. Wrong emergency protocols (the German Rettungsdienst system has specific algorithms)

---

## Month 4: Take the Kenntnisprüfung

By now you should have studied for 6-8 weeks straight. The exam itself is usually one day.

**What to bring:**
- Your ID
- Confirmation letter from the Kammer
- Stethoscope (for the practical part)
- Watch with second hand

**During the exam:**
- Speak slowly and clearly in German
- Always mention patient safety first
- If you don't know something, say what you would do to find out
- Follow the standard German clinical workflow: Anamnese → Untersuchung → Diagnostik → Therapie

**Results** come in 2-4 weeks. If you pass, congratulations — you're eligible for your Approbation. If you fail, you can retake once (some states allow two retakes).

---

## Month 5: Job Search

While waiting for the official Approbation certificate, start applying.

### Where to Find Jobs

- **Bundesärztekammer job board** — bundesaerztekammer.de
- **Ärzteblatt** — aerzteblatt.de/stellenmarkt
- **Indeed.de** — filter for "Arzt in Weiterbildung" or "Assistenzarzt"
- **Direct hospital websites** — many post jobs only on their own site
- **Recruiters** — Medical One, MH Corporate Health, OSO Expert

### Application Documents

German hospitals expect:
1. **Anschreiben** (cover letter) — one page, formal, explains why you want to work there
2. **Lebenslauf** (CV) — tabular format, with photo, no gaps unexplained
3. **Zeugnisse** (certificates) — all your degrees and language certs
4. **Referenzen** (references) — 2-3 contact persons from previous clinical work

### Interview Tips

- They will test your German clinical vocabulary
- Be ready to discuss your favorite clinical case
- Know what "Bereitschaftsdienst" (on-call duty) means — it's different from most countries
- Ask about "Fortbildungsmöglichkeiten" (training opportunities) — shows commitment

## Month 6: Relocation & Start

### Before You Arrive

- **Health insurance:** German public insurance (GKV) is mandatory. KVdL (Krankenversorgung der Landesverbände) is the scheme for doctors
- **Housing:** Start looking on Immowelt or WG-Gesucht 4-6 weeks before. Hospitals rarely provide housing
- **Visa:** With a job offer and pending Approbation, apply for a work visa or EU Blue Card
- **Bank account:** N26 or Comdirect can be opened online before you arrive

### First Week

1. Register your address (Anmeldung) at the Bürgeramt
2. Open a German bank account if not done
3. Get your social security number
4. Sign your work contract
5. Complete hospital onboarding (Betriebsärztliche Untersuchung, Datenschutz training)

### First Month as an Assistenzarzt

You will feel completely lost. This is normal. German clinical documentation (the Arztbrief) is unlike anything you've seen. Your prescriptions will need to be in German brand names. The hierarchy in German hospitals is more formal than in many other countries.

**Survival tips:**
- Ask a senior resident to read your first 10 Arztbriefe before you send them
- Learn the local computer system (SAP, Meona, or Orbis) in the first week
- Say yes to every opportunity to practice speaking German with patients
- Find one mentor — someone who can explain unwritten rules

## Final Checklist

- [ ] B2/C1 Goethe certificate
- [ ] All documents authenticated and translated
- [ ] Application submitted to Landesärztekammer
- [ ] Kenntnisprüfung scheduled
- [ ] Study plan for exam
- [ ] Hospital job applications sent
- [ ] Housing arranged
- [ ] Health insurance sorted
- [ ] Visa application (if non-EU)
- [ ] Bank account opened
