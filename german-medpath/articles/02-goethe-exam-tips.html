Goethe B2 vs C1 — What You Actually Need

A breakdown of German state requirements, exam differences, and the fastest path to passing.

The Short Answer

B2 is the minimum for Approbation in most states. But you need C1 or equivalent to pass the Fachsprachprüfung (medical German exam) and survive your first year as a doctor in Germany. Almost everyone who starts with B2 ends up studying to C1 anyway.

The Problem with B2 Only

B2 is enough to order food and have simple conversations. It is not enough to:

  • Take a patient history from a 75-year-old who speaks dialect
  • Write an Arztbrief that a German consultant will accept
  • Understand rapid-fire handoff during shift change
  • Explain a complex treatment plan to a worried family

Every IMG I have spoken to who started with B2 said the same thing: "I passed B2, I thought I was ready, and my first week on the ward destroyed me."

Fachsprachprüfung: The Real Target

The Fachsprachprüfung is administered by state medical chambers. It tests medical German specifically. You need approximately C1 level to pass.

What it covers: Taking patient history (Anamnese), physical examination vocabulary (Untersuchung), writing a medical report (Befunddokumentation), explaining diagnosis and treatment (Aufklärung), emergency communication (Notfall).

How to prepare: Use the Fachsprachprüfung standard textbook series, watch Medizin auf Deutsch on YouTube, practice with a tutor who specializes in medical German, and study the 200 most common clinical case presentations.
