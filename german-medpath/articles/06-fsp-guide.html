Fachsprachprüfung Germany: Complete Guide for Foreign Doctors

Everything you need to know about the German medical language exam. Format, preparation, state differences, and what examiners actually look for.

What Is the Fachsprachprüfung?

The Fachsprachprüfung (FSP) is the medical German language exam required for international doctors seeking the German Approbation. It tests your ability to communicate with patients and colleagues in clinical settings. It is not a general language test like Goethe. It tests clinical German, not grammatical precision.

Every international medical graduate from a non-EU country must pass the FSP before receiving their Approbation. Some EU graduates also need it depending on where they studied.

You need both. Goethe B2 or C1 proves you can function in German. The FSP proves you can treat patients in German.

Exam Format

The FSP takes about 45-60 minutes and has three parts:

Part 1: Patient History (Anamnese) — 15-20 minutes

You take a full history from a simulated patient (played by an actor). The examiner observes and evaluates:

  • Your ability to ask open-ended and closed questions
  • Whether you cover all relevant systems
  • How you handle a patient who is anxious or uncooperative
  • Your use of German clinical terminology

Common scenarios: Chest pain, abdominal pain, shortness of breath, headache, back pain, postoperative follow-up.

Part 2: Patient Communication (Aufklärung) — 15-20 minutes

You explain a diagnosis, procedure, or treatment plan to a patient. The examiner evaluates:

  • Clarity of explanation (can the patient understand you?)
  • Whether you mention risks and alternatives
  • How you handle questions or concerns
  • Empathy and bedside manner

Common scenarios: Explaining the need for surgery, discussing test results, obtaining informed consent, explaining a new diagnosis.

Part 3: Case Discussion (Arzt-Arzt-Gespräch) — 10-15 minutes

You present a case to the examiner as if they were a colleague. The examiner evaluates:

  • Structure of your presentation (does it follow the German clinical workflow?)
  • Clinical reasoning and differential diagnosis
  • Use of standard German medical abbreviations
  • Professional register and terminology

Expected structure: Vorstellung → Anamnese → Befund → Diagnose → Therapie → Prognose.

What Examiners Look For

Based on feedback from IMGs who have taken the FSP across multiple states, examiners consistently evaluate these factors:

  • Patient safety first. Always mention allergies, contraindications, and risks. This is non-negotiable.
  • Structured workflow. Follow the standard German clinical sequence. Examiners notice when you jump around.
  • Appropriate register. Use Sie with patients, du with colleagues only when invited. Formal register is always safe.
  • Explain in plain language. Patients in the exam may not understand medical terms. Your ability to simplify matters.
  • Handle the unexpected. If the simulated patient becomes emotional or difficult, how you respond is evaluated.

Preparation Strategy

Step 1: Assess Your Starting Point

If you have Goethe B2, plan 8-12 weeks of FSP preparation. If you have Goethe C1, plan 4-6 weeks. The FSP tests different skills from Goethe. Do not assume C1 means you will pass.

Step 2: Build Clinical Vocabulary

Focus on vocabulary organized by body system. Learn not just the terms but how to use them in sentences:

  • Cardiovascular: Palpitationen, thorakale Schmerzen, Belastungsdyspnoe
  • Respiratory: produktiver Husten, inspiratorischer Stridor, Giemen
  • Gastrointestinal: Oberbauchschmerzen, Hämatemesis, Teerstuhl
  • Neurology: Sensibilitätsstörungen, Paresen, Schwindel
  • Pharmacology: German drug names (Metamizol, ASS, Paracetamol, Ibuprofen)

Step 3: Practice Out Loud

The FSP is oral. Reading is not enough. You must practice speaking:

  • Record yourself taking a patient history in German (3-5 minutes)
  • Practice explaining a diagnosis to a family member in German
  • Find a study partner also preparing for the FSP and practice together
  • Use an iTalki or Preply tutor who specializes in medical German

Step 4: Master the Clinical Workflow

German medicine follows a specific workflow. Use it in every practice session:

  1. Vorstellung: "Darf ich mich vorstellen? Ich bin [Name], zuständige Ärztin / zuständiger Arzt."
  2. Anamnese: "Was hat Sie heute zu mir geführt?" → Current symptoms → Past history → Medications → Allergies → Social history
  3. Untersuchung: "Ich werde Sie jetzt untersuchen." Describe what you find.
  4. Diagnose: "Aufgrund der Befunde gehe ich von folgender Diagnose aus..."
  5. Therapie: "Ich empfehle folgende Behandlung..."
  6. Aufklärung: "Ich möchte Ihnen jetzt erklären, was wir als Nächstes tun werden."

Step 5: Mock Exams

Simulate the real exam conditions at least 3-4 times before your actual FSP. Have someone play the patient role. Time yourself. Record it and review later. Common mistakes that appear in mock exams: speaking too fast when nervous, skipping the allergy question, using English medical terms instead of German ones, and failing to structure the case presentation.

Common Reasons IMGs Fail the FSP

  • Insufficient vocabulary. You know general German but not clinical terms. This is the most common reason.
  • Poor structure. You jump between history, exam, and diagnosis without a clear flow.
  • Nervousness. The high-stakes setting affects your language production. Practice under pressure.
  • Wrong register. Using informal language with a patient or being too formal with a colleague.
  • Not asking about allergies. This is a critical safety step. Examiners specifically check for it.
  • Using English terms. Saying "hypertension" instead of "Hypertonie" or "surgery" instead of "Operation."
  • Talking too much or too little. Give enough detail but don't ramble. The exam is time-limited.

What to Do After You Pass

Passing the FSP is a major milestone but not the end. You still need to complete your Approbation process (or receive your Berufserlaubnis) and find a hospital position. Use your FSP preparation as confidence for your job interviews. The clinical vocabulary you learned will serve you daily.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take the FSP before I have my documents ready?

Usually not. Most Landesärztekammern require your Approbation application to be in process before scheduling the FSP. Check with your specific Kammer.

What happens if I fail?

You can retake the FSP. Most states allow one retake. Some allow two. The waiting period varies. Use the time to address specific weaknesses identified in your first attempt.

How long is the FSP certificate valid?

The FSP does not expire for Approbation purposes. However, if you take a job years later, some employers may ask for a more recent language assessment.

Is the FSP the same in every state?

The format is similar but the difficulty varies. Bayern and Baden-Württemberg are considered tougher. Sachsen and Thüringen are considered more forgiving.

Do I need the FSP if I studied medicine in German?

If you completed your medical degree at a German university, you are exempt. If you studied in another country but in German, it depends on the state. Check with your Landesärztekammer.
