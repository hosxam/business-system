# Goethe B2 vs C1 — Which One Do You Actually Need?

*A breakdown of German state requirements, exam differences, and the fastest path to passing for international medical graduates.*

---

## The Short Answer

**B2 is the minimum for Approbation in most states.** But you need **C1 or equivalent** to pass the Fachsprachprüfung (medical German exam) and survive your first year as a doctor in Germany. Almost everyone who starts with B2 ends up studying to C1 anyway.

## The Problem with B2 Only

B2 is enough to order food and have simple conversations. It is **not** enough to:

- Take a patient history in German with a 75-year-old who speaks dialect
- Write an Arztbrief (discharge summary) that a German consultant will accept
- Understand a rapid-fire handoff during shift change
- Explain a complex treatment plan to a worried family

Every IMG I've spoken to who started with B2 said the same thing: "I passed B2, I thought I was ready, and my first week on the ward destroyed me."

## Fastest Path to C1

### Option A: B2 → C1 in 4 months (recommended)

| Month | Focus | Hours per week |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| 1 | B2 grammar consolidation + 500 new words | 15 |
| 2 | C1 textbook (Aspekte Neu C1) + medical vocab | 15 |
| 3 | Exam technique + mock tests | 10 |
| 4 | Full mocks + speaking practice with tutor | 8 |

Total study time: ~200 hours from B2 to C1.

### Option B: B2 → C1 in 6 months (relaxed)

Same sequence but half intensity. Better if you're working while studying.

### Option C: Go directly to C1 (if you already have B1+)

Some intensive language schools in Germany offer C1-intensiv courses (8 weeks, 25 hours/week). This works if you can afford full-time study.

## Fachsprachprüfung: The Real Target

The Fachsprachprüfung is administered by state medical chambers. It tests medical German specifically. You need approximately C1 level to pass.

**What it covers:**
- Taking patient history (Anamnese)
- Physical examination vocabulary (Untersuchung)
- Writing a medical report (Befunddokumentation)
- Explaining diagnosis and treatment (Aufklärung)
- Emergency communication (Notfall)

**How to prepare:**
1. "Fachsprachprüfung – Erfolgreich bestehen" textbook series
2. Watch "Medizin auf Deutsch" YouTube channel
3. Practice with a tutor on iTalki (search for "medical German")
4. Study the 200 most common clinical cases and how to present them

---

## Documents You Need for Language Proof

- Goethe-Zertifikat B2 or C1 (most recognized)
- Telc Deutsch B2/C1 + Medizin (accepted in most states)
- TestDaF TDN 4 (equivalent to C1, accepted everywhere)
- DSH-2 or DSH-3 (if you took a German university course)

TestDaF and DSH scores are valid forever. Goethe certificates have no expiry but some Kammern want them from the last 2-3 years.
